# Spinoff worksheet: rules the skill reads

Source: Teaching 4, the instructor's worksheet, and Greenblatt, *You Can Be a Stock Market Genius* (Simon & Schuster), Ch. 3, "Chips Off the Old Stock" (cited as Ch. 3). Lines marked FILL are not supplied by Teaching 4 or Ch. 3 and are left blank on purpose; the skill stops and says so if any FILL is still blank.

## Every figure
- Cite it: filing, section, date.
- Tag it by basis: `historical`, `pro forma`, or `reported post-spin`. A Form 10 holds historical and pro forma side by side; never mix them in one calculation without saying so.
- One name, one file.

## Value range
Method: owner cash flow, valued as a range.
- Owner cash flow = after-tax earnings + depreciation and amortization − capital spending.
- Never a multiple of EBITDA alone. An EBITDA multiple may appear only as a cross-check beside the owner-cash-flow range.
- State the basis used for each input (pro forma for the first standalone year, unless a post-spin report exists).
- Cases, at least two:
  - Low case: owner cash flow ÷ 10% = value (10× owner cash flow). Assumption (Jackson), not from the book: a hurdle well above the Treasury yield, as a margin of safety.
  - Mid case: owner cash flow ÷ 7% = value (about 14× owner cash flow). Assumption (Jackson): a fair yield for an average business.
  - Owner cash flow input: the average of the last 3 fiscal years, which a Form 10 normally carries. Use the pro forma year where the Form 10 gives one, tagged as such. Assumption (Jackson).
- Negative owner cash flow (pre-revenue, cash-burning names): the method returns no operating value. Low case = net cash; mid case = Not known. Say so rather than substituting another method.
- Compare price on the worksheet date with the low case, as a percentage: (price − low) ÷ low.

## Client purchase size
- Sizing logic (Greenblatt, Ch. 2, on diversification; check against your copy): a concentrated portfolio of roughly 6–8 positions in different industries removes most of the risk that diversification can remove. So one position ≈ client portfolio ÷ 6 to 8.
- Client portfolio: $1,000,000. Placeholder assumption; replace with the client write-up's figure.
- Purchase size: $1,000,000 ÷ 8 positions = $125,000. Uses the top of Ch. 2's 6–8 range.
- Liquidity test: median daily dollar volume over the last 60 trading days (about 3 months), compared with the purchase size. Assumption (Jackson).
- Buyable if purchase size ≤ 10% of median daily dollar volume per day, completed within 3 trading days. Assumption (Jackson): keeps the order from moving a thinly traded spinoff's price.

## Spinoff window
- A name stays on the screen until 12 months after its distribution date. Assumption (Jackson), from Ch. 3's point that the selling by institutions that don't want the shares is concentrated after the spin.
- If not buyable at the size: say so in "Can you buy it" and give no conviction.

## Conviction rule (Teaching 4 tests, organized by Ch. 3's three signs of a promising spinoff)
Ch. 3 says to look for spinoffs where (a) institutions don't want the shares, for reasons unrelated to the business's merits; (b) insiders do want them, through a real stake or incentives tied to the spinoff's own stock; and (c) the spin reveals a bargain or a good business that the parent's structure was hiding.

High conviction, all of:
1. (a) An institutional or indifferent seller identified, with its citation (index exclusion, size, mismatch with the parent's holders, a holder selling for reasons outside the business).
2. (b) Insiders' interest shown, with its citation: open-market purchases after distribution, or a meaningful stake or option grant in the spinoff's own stock disclosed in the Form 10 or proxy.
3. (c) Price at or below the low case.
4. Nothing the parent pushed into it is unquantified. Ch. 3 notes that debt loaded onto a spinoff can be the source of upside rather than only risk, so debt does not lower conviction once it is quantified and covered.
5. Buyable at the client's purchase size.

Low conviction: any of 1–4 missing.
No conviction: not buyable at size, or a required figure has no citation.

Ch. 3 also points to the parent after the spin and to partial spinoffs and rights offerings as places to look. Those inform "The parent after the spin" but do not set conviction.

## What the parent pushed into it
For each item (debt, pension, legal or environmental liability, supply or transition agreements at off-market terms): amount, citation. If none: "looked in [sections], found none."

## Not known
Every gap written as a line, never filled by estimate.
